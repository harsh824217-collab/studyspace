📋 Exam Patterns & Syllabus (परीक्षा पैटर्न)

🚂 Railway (RRB) Exams

RRB ALP (Assistant Loco Pilot)

CBT 1 Exam Pattern: The first stage is a 60-minute computer-based test comprising 75 objective questions. It covers Mathematics (20 questions), General Intelligence & Reasoning (25 questions), General Science (20 questions), and General Awareness & Current Affairs (10 questions). There is a negative marking of 1/3rd for each wrong answer. This stage is qualifying in nature.

CBT 2 Exam Pattern: Stage two is divided into Part A and Part B, with a total duration of 2 hours and 30 minutes. Part A (90 minutes, 100 questions) decides your merit and tests Math, Reasoning, and Basic Science & Engineering. Part B (60 minutes, 75 questions) is a qualifying Trade Test specific to the candidate's technical background.

RRB Technician (Grade 1 Signal)

Exam Pattern: The Technician Grade 1 (Signal) exam requires candidates to attempt 100 questions in 90 minutes. The syllabus is uniquely structured compared to other railway exams. It heavily focuses on Basic Science & Engineering (35 questions) and Basics of Computers & Applications (20 questions). Additionally, it includes Mathematics (20 questions), General Intelligence & Reasoning (15 questions), and General Awareness (10 questions). A 1/3rd negative marking applies.

RRB Technician (Grade 3)

Exam Pattern: For Technician Grade 3 posts, the computer-based test also consists of 100 questions to be solved in 90 minutes. However, the subject weightage is different. The highest weightage is given to General Science (40 questions). This is followed by Mathematics (25 questions), General Intelligence & Reasoning (25 questions), and General Awareness (10 questions). A standard 1/3rd negative marking rule is implemented.

RRB JE (Junior Engineer)

CBT 1 Exam Pattern: The Junior Engineer Tier 1 exam is a 90-minute test with 100 questions. It is designed to screen candidates based on Mathematics (30 questions), General Intelligence & Reasoning (25 questions), General Awareness (15 questions), and General Science (30 questions).

CBT 2 Exam Pattern: The second tier is a 120-minute comprehensive exam containing 150 questions. While it has sections for General Awareness, Physics, Chemistry, and Computer Basics, the majority of the paper—100 out of 150 questions—is dedicated strictly to Technical Abilities directly related to the candidate's specific engineering discipline.

RRB NTPC (Non-Technical Popular Categories)

CBT 1 Exam Pattern: The first stage computer-based test is common for all notified posts of NTPC. It consists of 100 questions to be completed in 90 minutes. The sections are General Awareness (40 questions), Mathematics (30 questions), and General Intelligence & Reasoning (30 questions).

CBT 2 Exam Pattern: Candidates shortlisted for CBT 2 will face 120 questions in 90 minutes. The difficulty level is scaled up according to the post level (Levels 2 to 6). The syllabus structure remains the same but with increased questions: General Awareness (50 questions), Mathematics (35 questions), and General Intelligence & Reasoning (35 questions). Depending on the post, candidates may then face a Typing Skill Test or Computer Based Aptitude Test (CBAT).

🏛️ SSC (Staff Selection Commission)

SSC CGL (Combined Graduate Level)

Tier 1 Exam Pattern: The preliminary exam is 60 minutes long and carries 200 marks for 100 questions. It is divided equally into four sections: General Intelligence & Reasoning (25 questions), General Awareness (25 questions), Quantitative Aptitude (25 questions), and English Comprehension (25 questions). There is a negative marking of 0.50 marks per wrong answer.

Tier 2 Exam Pattern: The main exam is divided into distinct sections and modules tested in a single session. Session-I (2 hours 15 minutes) covers Mathematical Abilities, Reasoning, English Language, and General Awareness, followed immediately by a Computer Knowledge Module. Session-II includes a Data Entry Speed Test (Typing) which is qualifying in nature.

SSC CHSL (Combined Higher Secondary Level)

Tier 1 Exam Pattern: Similar to CGL but designed for the 10+2 level. The exam consists of 100 questions for 200 marks to be completed in 60 minutes. The sections are English Language, General Intelligence, Quantitative Aptitude (Basic Arithmetic Skill), and General Awareness. Negative marking is 0.50 marks.

Tier 2 Exam Pattern: Tier 2 features objective tests for Mathematics, Reasoning, English, and General Awareness, alongside a Computer test. Most crucially, it includes a mandatory Skill Test or Typing Test for Data Entry Operators, LDC, and JSA posts.

SSC MTS (Multi-Tasking Staff)

Exam Pattern: The MTS exam has been revamped into a single computer-based examination divided into two mandatory sessions. Session-I covers Numerical and Mathematical Ability (20 questions) and Reasoning Ability (20 questions) with a 45-minute limit and importantly, no negative marking. Session-II covers General Awareness (25 questions) and English Language (25 questions) within 45 minutes, where a negative marking of 1 mark for each wrong answer applies. Final merit is based strictly on Session-II scores.

👮‍♂️ Bihar State Exams (BPSC & Police)

BPSC (Bihar Public Service Commission)

Prelims Exam Pattern: The BPSC Civil Services preliminary exam consists of a single General Studies paper. It has 150 objective-type questions carrying 150 marks, with a duration of 2 hours. A negative marking of 1/3rd is applied. It covers Indian History, Geography, Polity, Economy, Bihar-specific knowledge, and Science.

Mains Exam Pattern: The Mains exam consists of a qualifying General Hindi paper (100 marks), General Studies Paper-1 (300 marks), General Studies Paper-2 (300 marks), an Essay Paper (300 marks), and an optional subject which is now qualifying and MCQ-based. Candidates who clear the mains undergo a 120-mark personal interview.

CSBC (Bihar Police Constable)

Written Exam Pattern: The CSBC Constable selection starts with an OMR-based written examination. The exam contains 100 objective questions for 100 marks, to be solved in 2 hours. The syllabus includes Hindi, English, Mathematics, Social Science (History, Geography, Civics, Economics), and Science (Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Botany).

Physical Test (PET): It is crucial to note that the written test is only qualifying in nature (minimum 30% marks required). The final merit list is prepared purely on the basis of marks obtained in the Physical Endurance Test (PET), which evaluates performance in the 1.6 km Run, Shot Put throw, and High Jump.
